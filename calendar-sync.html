Calendar Synchronisation Investigation

This investigation demonstrates my approach to analysing third-party integration issues where the reported behaviour could not immediately be attributed to either the SaaS platform or the external provider. The objective was to understand the synchronisation workflow, gather technical evidence and determine where the issue originated before escalating further.

Investigation Summary

System Calendar Synchronisation
Issue Type Third-Party Integration Investigation
Severity Medium
Customer Impact Calendar events were not synchronising as expected between systems.
Investigation Support Engineering
Outcome Investigation identified limitations of the existing integration and informed recommendations for future improvement.

Background

A customer reported that calendar events created within the platform were not synchronising in the way they expected with their external calendar. As multiple systems were involved, it was initially unclear whether the behaviour was caused by platform configuration, integration design or limitations of the third-party calendar provider.

Understanding exactly how synchronisation behaved was important before escalating the issue, ensuring Engineering received evidence rather than assumptions.

Objective

Investigate the calendar synchronisation workflow, reproduce the reported behaviour, compare expected and actual functionality and establish whether the issue represented a software defect, configuration issue or integration limitation.

Investigation Timeline

  1. Reviewed the customer's reported synchronisation behaviour.
  2. Verified current calendar configuration and integration settings.
  3. Researched existing product documentation and supported functionality.
  4. Tested synchronisation behaviour across multiple calendar scenarios.
  5. Compared expected behaviour with actual synchronisation results.
  6. Discussed findings with Product and Engineering.
  7. Investigated alternative synchronisation approaches used by third-party providers such as Cronofy.
  8. Documented conclusions and recommendations.

Investigation

The investigation focused on understanding the complete synchronisation workflow rather than assuming a software defect.

  • Reviewed customer expectations against documented functionality.
  • Tested multiple synchronisation scenarios.
  • Compared one-way and two-way calendar behaviour.
  • Researched industry approaches to calendar integrations.
  • Evaluated whether behaviour aligned with current product design.
  • Documented findings with supporting evidence for Product and Engineering.

Evidence Collected

Testing confirmed that the observed behaviour was consistent with the current integration design rather than an intermittent platform failure. The investigation highlighted opportunities to improve synchronisation capabilities through more advanced calendar integration technology.

Evidence collected included:

  • Documented customer workflow.
  • Configuration review.
  • Expected versus actual synchronisation behaviour.
  • Comparison against product documentation.
  • Technical research into third-party calendar providers.
  • Recommendations for future investigation.

Technologies Used

WriteUpp Calendar Integration Google Calendar Microsoft Outlook Cronofy Research Manual Testing

Outcome

The investigation demonstrated that the reported behaviour aligned with the current integration design rather than indicating a production defect. By eliminating potential causes and documenting the findings, the investigation reduced uncertainty and provided Product and Engineering with a clear understanding of the customer's requirements.

The investigation also broadened my understanding of calendar APIs, synchronisation models and third-party scheduling platforms, leading me to explore providers such as Cronofy and how dedicated calendar infrastructure differs from basic calendar integrations.

Skills Demonstrated

  • Integration troubleshooting.
  • Root cause analysis.
  • Technical research.
  • API and calendar integration awareness.
  • Customer issue ownership.
  • Technical documentation.
  • Cross-functional collaboration.

Technical Reflection

Not every investigation results in identifying a software defect. Sometimes the most valuable outcome is confidently demonstrating why a system behaves as designed, eliminating unnecessary development work and identifying opportunities for future product improvements. This investigation reinforced the importance of understanding third-party integrations before drawing conclusions.

Why this investigation matters

This investigation demonstrates my approach to Support Engineering: understanding customer requirements, analysing integration behaviour, gathering technical evidence, collaborating with Product and Engineering teams and communicating findings that support informed technical decisions.
