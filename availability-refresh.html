Online Booking Availability Refresh Investigation

This started with a fairly simple question: why wasn't a change to appointment availability showing up in Online Booking?

Customers were making changes to clinician availability within the main platform, but patients could sometimes continue seeing the old availability when trying to book online.

I wanted to work out where the disconnect was happening, whether it could be reproduced consistently, and what was actually causing the delay.

At a Glance

System Online Booking
Type Availability / refresh behaviour
Impact Patients could see outdated appointment availability.
Tools Browser Developer Tools, Jira, Raygun and manual testing.
Outcome Engineering identified the condition that prevented the refresh from being triggered.

What Was Happening?

Customers reported that changes to clinician availability or appointment slots were not always appearing immediately in the online booking portal.

The interesting part was that the change had been saved successfully in the main platform. There was no obvious error. The internal schedule looked correct, but Online Booking could still be showing the previous state.

That raised a few possibilities. Was there a synchronisation delay? Was the booking service caching the previous state? Was something failing when the schedule changed? Or was the refresh simply not being triggered?

The next step was to try to reproduce it rather than guessing.

How I Investigated It

I worked through the booking workflow and compared what was happening in the main platform with what was being displayed in Online Booking.

1
Created test availability

I created and modified appointment availability so I could compare the behaviour under controlled conditions.

2
Compared both sides

I checked the updated availability in the main platform and then compared it with what was being presented through Online Booking.

3
Repeated the tests

I tested different appointment types, locations and configuration changes to see whether the behaviour was consistent.

4
Looked underneath the UI

Browser Developer Tools were used to observe requests and understand what was happening when availability changed and when the booking page refreshed.

5
Documented what I found

I recorded timestamps, screenshots, reproduction steps and observations so the behaviour could be investigated by Engineering without having to start from scratch.

6
Worked with Engineering

The investigation was used to reproduce the behaviour and narrow down the conditions under which the refresh wasn't happening.

The Interesting Bit

During testing, I found a workaround that consistently forced the availability to refresh.

If the clinician was removed from Online Booking availability and then enabled again, the updated availability appeared correctly.

The clue

The workaround didn't change the underlying working hours. It effectively forced the Online Booking configuration to be saved again — which suggested that the problem wasn't with the availability itself, but with what was triggering the refresh.

That gave us something much more useful than simply saying "availability sometimes takes a while to update." There was now a repeatable behaviour that could be tested.

Then It Happened Again

Several months later, another customer reported what initially looked like a separate issue.

The symptoms were familiar.

  • Availability had been changed successfully.
  • The main platform showed the new schedule.
  • Online Booking continued showing the old availability.
  • The same workaround resolved the problem.

Because the original investigation had been documented, I was able to compare the new case with the previous one rather than treating it as an entirely new problem.

The reproduction steps and workaround matched closely enough to link the two incidents together.

That second occurrence turned out to be important because it showed that this wasn't just an isolated timing problem.

What Was Eventually Found

Engineering determined that the refresh depended on how the clinician's working hours had been changed.

When a clinician updated their own working hours, the Online Booking refresh process detected the change correctly.

However, when the same working hours were modified by a site administrator, the refresh event wasn't being triggered in the same way.

The result was that the underlying schedule was correct, but Online Booking continued displaying the previous availability.

Confirmed root cause

Administrator-initiated changes to clinician working hours were not triggering the Online Booking availability refresh. Saving the Online Booking configuration manually forced the refresh, which explained why the workaround consistently fixed the problem.

What I Kept Track Of

A useful part of this investigation was making sure there was enough evidence for someone else to pick up the problem without having to repeat all of the exploratory work.

Reproduction Steps

A repeatable sequence showing how the behaviour could be produced.

Comparisons

Examples of what the main platform showed versus what appeared in Online Booking.

Timing

Timestamps showing when changes were made and when the updated availability became visible.

Browser Evidence

Observations from Developer Tools and the requests made during the booking workflow.

Where It Ended Up

The combination of the original investigation, the later matching incident and the repeatable workaround helped Engineering identify the specific condition that was preventing the refresh.

The issue could then be addressed at the source rather than relying on the manual workaround.

For me, one of the useful lessons from this investigation was how much easier a difficult production problem becomes when you can turn a vague report into a repeatable sequence of events.

What I Took From It

This was a good example of why I enjoy troubleshooting. The initial report was essentially "the availability hasn't updated", but the useful answer required working through the whole chain of events and finding out exactly what was different between a successful update and an unsuccessful one.

The other lesson was the value of keeping good investigation notes. When the same behaviour appeared months later, the original evidence made it possible to recognise the pattern quickly instead of starting again from zero.
