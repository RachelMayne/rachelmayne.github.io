Online Booking Edge Case Investigation

This investigation demonstrates my approach to analysing customer-reported issues within a live SaaS platform. The objective was to identify a repeatable pattern, gather technical evidence and provide Engineering with sufficient information to investigate the reported behaviour.

Investigation Summary

System Online Booking
Issue Type Booking Workflow Edge Case
Severity High
Customer Impact Patients were unable to complete valid online bookings.
Investigation Support Engineering
Outcome Engineering successfully reproduced the issue and released a production fix.

Background

A customer reported that appointments could not always be booked online despite appointment availability appearing to exist. The behaviour was inconsistent and could not initially be reproduced, suggesting an edge case rather than a general platform failure.

As Online Booking is a customer-facing feature, understanding the cause of the issue was important to minimise disruption and restore confidence in the booking experience.

Objective

Determine why valid appointments were being rejected by the Online Booking system, establish reliable reproduction steps and provide Engineering with clear technical evidence for investigation.

Investigation Timeline

  1. Customer reported intermittent booking failures.
  2. Recreated the customer's booking configuration.
  3. Tested multiple appointment types, practitioners and booking dates.
  4. Compared successful and unsuccessful booking attempts.
  5. Repeated testing using different availability scenarios.
  6. Identified the interaction between booking restrictions and appointment availability.
  7. Documented findings within Jira.
  8. Engineering reproduced the issue and deployed a production fix.

Investigation

The investigation focused on recreating the customer's environment while systematically eliminating possible causes.

  • Recreated the customer's Online Booking configuration.
  • Tested multiple appointment types and practitioner schedules.
  • Compared successful and unsuccessful booking attempts.
  • Repeated testing using different booking dates and appointment availability.
  • Reviewed the interaction between booking restrictions and appointment availability.
  • Documented every scenario until reliable reproduction steps were established.

Evidence Collected

Testing identified a repeatable edge case where the configured 24-hour booking restriction interacted unexpectedly with appointment availability. Under specific conditions the booking engine rejected appointments that should have been available.

Evidence collected included:

  • Step-by-step reproduction instructions.
  • Expected versus actual behaviour.
  • Screenshots demonstrating the issue.
  • Configuration details.
  • Assessment of customer impact.
  • Technical notes for Engineering.

Outcome

Providing structured evidence enabled Engineering to reproduce the defect quickly, prioritise the issue and implement an out-of-hours production fix, restoring normal booking functionality.

Although I was not responsible for implementing the software fix, providing clear technical documentation reduced investigation time and supported effective collaboration between Support, Product and Engineering.

Skills Demonstrated

  • Technical troubleshooting within a live SaaS platform.
  • Structured bug reproduction.
  • Root cause investigation.

Technical Reflection

This investigation reinforced the importance of reproducing customer workflows exactly as reported. Seemingly intermittent issues are often caused by edge cases that only appear under specific configuration combinations. Structured testing and clear documentation reduced Engineering investigation time and supported a faster production resolution.

Why this investigation matters

This investigation demonstrates my approach to Support Engineering: understanding customer impact, reproducing software defects, gathering evidence, collaborating with Engineering teams and documenting findings that enable efficient issue resolution.
