Appointment Portal Edit Investigation

This investigation demonstrates my approach to troubleshooting a customer-facing portal issue by distinguishing between configuration problems and a genuine platform defect.

Investigation Summary

SystemPatient Appointment Portal
Issue TypePortal Appointment Editing
SeverityMedium
Customer ImpactPatients could cancel appointments but could not edit them through the portal.
InvestigationSupport Engineering
OutcomeEvidence linked affected sites to an existing Engineering defect involving legacy data.

Background

Several customers reported that the Edit Appointment option was unavailable even though appointment cancellation continued to work normally.

The initial challenge was determining whether the behaviour was caused by incorrect customer configuration or an underlying product defect.

Objective

Establish whether the issue was configuration-related or a platform defect by reproducing the behaviour and comparing affected environments.

Investigation Timeline

  1. Reviewed customer reports.
  2. Reproduced the behaviour across multiple customer sites.
  3. Compared working and affected portal configurations.
  4. Verified portal settings and appointment behaviour.
  5. Reviewed existing Engineering investigations.
  6. Matched findings with a known defect.
  7. Provided evidence supporting Engineering analysis.

Investigation

  • Reproduced the issue across multiple customer sites.
  • Compared working versus broken configurations.
  • Verified portal settings and appointment behaviour.
  • Confirmed configuration was consistent across environments.
  • Linked investigation findings to existing Jira tickets.
  • Documented reproducible evidence for Engineering.

Evidence Collected

  • Configuration comparisons.
  • Reproduction steps.
  • Portal behaviour observations.
  • Customer impact assessment.
  • Engineering ticket references.

Root Cause Analysis

The investigation confirmed that customer configuration, portal permissions and appointment settings were consistent across both working and affected sites. Extensive testing demonstrated that the inability to edit appointments could not be reproduced by changing configuration alone, indicating that the issue originated within the application rather than individual customer environments.

Working closely with the Head of Product, I reproduced the behaviour across multiple customer sites and systematically compared legacy and newer environments. By validating portal configuration, appointment behaviour and customer settings, we eliminated a wide range of potential causes before identifying a consistent pattern affecting older legacy sites.

Comparative testing showed that the issue was isolated to specific legacy environments despite identical configuration. Documenting reproducible evidence and linking the findings to existing Engineering investigations enabled the team to focus on the underlying application behaviour rather than continuing to investigate customer configuration or user error.

Although the software fix was implemented by Engineering, the investigation significantly reduced diagnosis time by ruling out configuration issues, confirming the behaviour across multiple customer sites and narrowing the defect to a legacy platform issue involving NULL value handling.

Confirmed Root Cause

The investigation confirmed that the issue was not caused by portal configuration or appointment settings. Engineering identified the underlying cause as a legacy platform defect involving NULL value handling, which prevented the Edit Appointment functionality from becoming available on affected older customer sites.
