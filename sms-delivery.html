Jersey SMS Routing Investigation

This investigation demonstrates my approach to diagnosing a complex third-party SMS routing issue affecting Jersey mobile numbers. Rather than assuming the application was at fault, I investigated provider behaviour, regional numbering plans and carrier availability before identifying the true cause.

Background

Customers reported that SMS reminders consistently failed for Jersey mobile numbers even though standard UK mobile numbers continued to work successfully.

Because Jersey shares the +44 country code, the behaviour initially appeared to be an application defect. I suspected the issue could instead relate to regional routing and investigated the delivery path beyond the SaaS platform.

Objective

Determine whether the fault existed within the application, the SMS provider or regional telecommunications routing, then provide Engineering and Product with sufficient technical evidence.

Investigation

Testing confirmed the platform generated SMS messages successfully. Evidence showed failures only occurred for Jersey mobile numbers. I collected affected numbers, compared provider behaviour and challenged the assumption that every +44 number should be treated identically.

Root Cause Analysis

The investigation confirmed that the SaaS platform was successfully generating and submitting SMS messages for delivery. Testing demonstrated that failures only occurred for Jersey mobile numbers, while standard UK mobile numbers continued to receive messages successfully.

By comparing affected numbers, researching regional numbering plans and escalating the issue directly with Bandwidth, I established that the failure did not originate within the application. Bandwidth confirmed that they had no supported carrier coverage for Jersey, despite Jersey sharing the +44 country code with the United Kingdom.

Further investigation showed that another provider, Twilio, handled the same numbering ranges differently. This highlighted that the issue was caused by provider-specific routing rather than application logic.

Working alongside Product, we discussed a future enhancement that would identify Jersey (and later Isle of Man) mobile prefixes and route those messages through an alternative SMS provider. Although this was outside the scope of the immediate incident, the investigation provided the technical evidence required to support that future improvement.

Confirmed Root Cause

SMS delivery failures were caused by missing carrier coverage within the third-party SMS provider for Jersey mobile networks. The application generated messages correctly; the failure occurred after the messages left the platform during external telecommunications routing.

Outcome

The investigation demonstrated the application was functioning correctly and that the underlying issue was regional carrier coverage within the third-party SMS provider. The findings supported future product improvements and provided customers with an explanation and workaround.

My investigation reduced Engineering effort by identifying the external dependency before development resources were spent investigating the application itself.

Technical Reflection

This investigation reinforced the importance of challenging assumptions. Although Jersey uses the +44 country code, carrier availability differed from mainland UK. Following the evidence rather than the assumption led directly to the true root cause.

Why this investigation matters

This case study demonstrates analytical troubleshooting beyond application support by combining SaaS investigation, telecommunications research, supplier management and structured evidence gathering to identify a genuine external root cause.
