Xero Bulk Export Investigation

This investigation demonstrates my approach to diagnosing a financial integration issue affecting bulk invoice exports. The objective was to determine why exports failed under specific conditions, gather technical evidence and provide Engineering with sufficient information to investigate and resolve the behaviour.

Background

Customers reported that individual invoices exported successfully, while larger batches failed or completed only partially.

Because accounting exports are business-critical, identifying the cause quickly was essential to minimise disruption.

Objective

Identify the conditions causing bulk export failures, establish reliable reproduction steps and provide Engineering with structured technical evidence.

Investigation

Root Cause Analysis

The investigation confirmed that the SaaS platform was successfully generating and submitting SMS messages for delivery. Testing demonstrated that failures only occurred for Jersey mobile numbers, while standard UK mobile numbers continued to receive messages successfully.

By comparing affected numbers, researching regional numbering plans and escalating the issue directly with Bandwidth, I established that the failure did not originate within the application. Bandwidth confirmed that it did not provide carrier coverage for Jersey mobile networks, despite Jersey sharing the +44 country code with the United Kingdom.

Further investigation showed that another SMS provider, Twilio, handled the same numbering ranges differently. This confirmed that the issue resulted from provider-specific routing behaviour rather than application logic.

Working alongside Product, I proposed a future enhancement that would identify Jersey (and later Isle of Man) mobile prefixes and automatically route those messages through an alternative SMS provider. Although this was outside the scope of the immediate incident, the investigation supplied the technical evidence required to support future development.

Confirmed Root Cause

SMS delivery failures were caused by missing carrier coverage within the third-party SMS provider for Jersey mobile networks. The application generated messages correctly; the failure occurred after messages left the platform during external telecommunications routing.
